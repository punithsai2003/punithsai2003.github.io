Machine Learning · Live App

IPL Win Probability Predictor

This app shows a chasing team's chance of winning at any moment of an IPL run chase the same kind of live win-probability you see on TV. It learned from 138,000+ match situations across 1,100+ IPL matches.

An early version looked too good (99% accurate). I found the cause; the model was accidentally peeking at answers in advance (a mistake called data leakage) and fixed it by only testing on whole matches it had never seen. The honest scores are below.

What it revealed: a team needing about 120 off the last 10 overs wins only 22% of the time far lower than most fans would guess.

Player-Level ML · Decision Tool

Death-Over Matchup Advisor

This tool answers the toughest call in a T20 death over: who should bowl, and to which batter? You give it a batter, two bowling options and the match situation, and it shows the risk for each choice plus a suggested matchup.

It learned from real IPL and international T20 history, and is built so the model only ever uses what happened before each ball never future information. Single overs are naturally unpredictable, so it's honest about its edge (AUC ~0.64): a real tilt in your favour, not a guarantee.

Power BI · Selection Analytics a 4-part data story

India's Best T20 XV — Built From 62,000 Deliveries

One question drove this report: if you only looked at the data, who would you pick? No reputations, just 269 international T20s and ~62,000 balls. Instead of judging a player's whole innings, I split every player across the three parts of a T20. Powerplay, Middle Overs and Death and let each part pick its own specialists. The four pages below tell that story in order: three pieces of evidence, then the final team.

Page 01 · Powerplay

Overs 1–6: the intent era

The first six overs are all about attacking while the field is up. India's own numbers tell the story; a powerplay strike rate of 147.8 and 56 runs on average; the old "start slow" opener is gone; India now picks players who attack from ball one.

One name stands out: Abhishek Sharma, scoring at a huge 192 strike rate over 47 innings; a big enough sample to trust. Tilak Varma, Sanju Samson and Ishan Kishan round out an attacking top order. The bowling is thinner: Arshdeep Singh takes the most powerplay wickets (42), but India has only one truly economical new-ball bowler Jasprit Bumrah at 6.35.

Page 02 · Middle Overs

Overs 7–16: where the game is quietly won

Overs 7–16 are less about power and more about scoring steadily without losing wickets. The batting core shows up here: Shivam Dube averages 61.4, and Sanju Samson combines a 46.6 average with a fast 172 strike rate someone who can hold the innings together and still attack. That one finding reshaped the whole batting order.

With the ball, this phase belongs to spin. The four leading wicket-takers are all spinners Varun (58), Axar (43), Kuldeep (40) and Bishnoi (29) each going for under 7.9 an over. This page also sets up the report's boldest call: even though Suryakumar Yadav plays the most middle overs (48 innings), his 33.3 average trails Samson, Pandya and Tilak in the very phase he's known for.

Page 03 · Death Overs

Overs 17–20: the nerve test

The last four overs are pure specialism scoring races up (team strike rate 168.8) and bowling gets expensive (8.83 an over), so every run and every dot ball counts double. Two finishers stand clear: Rinku Singh (196 strike rate) and Shivam Dube (187).

The bowling call is the most careful one. Bumrah is outstanding just 6.43 an over at the death but he can't bowl every over. Arshdeep Singh takes the most death wickets (37) at a higher 9.31, and this is exactly why my selection rule rewarded taking wickets to make up for costlier overs, not just economy.

Page 04 · The Verdict

The Final XV — every pick defended by a number

The final page turns three phases of evidence into one squad of 15 players, each picked for a clear stat rather than a reputation. The core: Abhishek Sharma and Sanju Samson to open, Tilak Varma and Ishan Kishan in the top order, Hardik Pandya plus finishers Rinku Singh and Shivam Dube, a spin trio of Axar, Varun and Kuldeep, and a pace pair of Bumrah and Arshdeep.

The boldest call: Suryakumar Yadav misses out; his middle-overs average (33.3) is behind Samson (46.6), Pandya (44.4) and Tilak (40.8). I've also been upfront about the limits: this uses international T20 data only (not IPL form or fitness), and every cut-off was set from India's own averages, not gut feel ; which is how it surfaced a real gap: only one genuinely economical powerplay bowler.

Virat Kohli ICC Tournament Performance Dashboard

An interactive look at Kohli's career in ICC events; World Cups 2011–2023, the 2025 Champions Trophy and recent series set up as a 2027 World Cup selection case. The headline cards say it all: 48 innings, 2,555 runs, a 53.2 average, 91.5 strike rate and 9 hundreds. You can filter by opponent, year, venue and how he got out.

The why

It started with loving the game

I fell for cricket long before I fell for data and that love is exactly what pulled me into SQL, Python, Power BI and machine learning.

Every skill on this page was learned in service of a question I actually cared about: why did that chase collapse? who should have bowled the 19th? does this player really deserve the XI? Chasing honest answers to cricket questions taught me to write window functions, parse ball-by-ball JSON, engineer leakage-safe features and calibrate a model until I trusted it.

That's the way I like to work a real-world passion driving the technical skill, so the analysis never loses touch with the decision it's meant to serve. Sport is where I learned data. It's the kind of work I want to keep doing.
